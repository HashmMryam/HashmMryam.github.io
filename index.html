How a polyurethane coating changes sand

I study binary mixtures of coarse and fine silica sand in which one or both fractions are coated with a thin layer of polyurethane, and how that coating changes the soil’s dynamic and mechanical behaviour.

The mixtures range from pure coarse to pure fine sand, with coarse–fine blends in between. Each blend is tested uncoated as a baseline, and then with the coarse grains, the fine grains, or both coated. All specimens are prepared to the same relative density so that the coating is the only change.

Small-strain stiffness and damping come from resonant column tests; strength comes from consolidated-undrained triaxial tests; scanning electron microscopy checks how the coating covers each grain.

  • Resonant columnShear modulus and damping curves at 100, 200 and 300 kPa confinement
  • CU triaxialStress–strain and pore pressure response at 100, 200 and 300 kPa
  • SEM imagingCoating coverage and bonding between grains
  • MaterialsSilica sand, grade 4 (coarse) and grade 7 (fine); relative density 60%

Pixel-perfect depth on SEM images

Presented “Pixel-Perfect Visual Geometry Estimation” (IEEE TPAMI, 2026), a diffusion transformer for clean 3D point clouds. I ran it on SEM images of my coated sand: edges came out clean, but grains turned into flat sheets, a clear case of distribution shift.

Undamped forced vibration analysis of castellated steel beam with circular, square and pentagonal web openings

Maryam Hashm, Masihullah Noori, Ahmad Reshad Noori, Silda Ghazi Doori

Adıyaman Üniversitesi Mühendislik Bilimleri Dergisi (Adıyaman University Journal of Engineering Sciences), vol. 11, no. 22, pp. 46–62, April 2024

Castellated steel beams with three web opening shapes were modelled with the finite element method in ANSYS and loaded with time-varying forces. The opening shape had a significant influence on the vibration amplitudes and oscillation periods of the beams.
